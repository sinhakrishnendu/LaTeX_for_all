\chapter{Advanced Mathematical Writing}
\label{chap:advanced-math}
\index{mathematics!advanced}\index{matrix}\index{calculus}\index{theorem}

This optional chapter is for documents that need derivations, piecewise
definitions, matrices, calculus, or theorem-like statements. Readers whose
work uses only short equations may continue to quantities and units and return
here when a project requires these structures.

\begin{learningoutcomes}
  \item Break a long derivation into aligned, readable lines.
  \item Typeset cases, matrices, and vectors.
  \item Write derivatives, integrals, sums, and limits.
  \item Define theorem-like environments with \pkg{amsthm}.
  \item Produce a definition, theorem, and proof with consistent numbering.
\end{learningoutcomes}

\section{Break mathematics at relationships}

A long equation should not be squeezed by reducing its type size. Break it at a
meaningful relation and use \code{aligned} inside one numbered equation when
the lines form one logical statement:

\begin{latexcode}{One numbered multi-line derivation}
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \sum_{i=1}^{n}(d_i-\bar d)^2
    &= \sum_{i=1}^{n}(d_i^2-2d_i\bar d+\bar d^2) \\
    &= \sum_{i=1}^{n}d_i^2-n\bar d^2.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\end{latexcode}

The outer environment supplies one number; \code{aligned} supplies alignment
without separate numbers. Use \code{align} instead when individual lines need
separate labels or stand as distinct equations.

\begin{pausepredict}
If the outer \code{equation} is replaced by \code{\textbackslash[ ...
\textbackslash]}, will the derivation keep a visible number? Predict, then test.
\end{pausepredict}

\section{Cases and matrices}

The \code{cases} environment creates a left brace and two aligned columns:

\begin{equation}
  I_i =
  \begin{cases}
    1, & \text{if both simulated values are present},\\
    0, & \text{otherwise}.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:complete-pair-indicator}
\end{equation}

Matrix environments differ mainly in delimiters:

\begin{latexcode}{Matrices and a vector}
\[
  \mathbf{X} =
  \begin{bmatrix}
    1 & A_1 \\
    1 & A_2 \\
    \vdots & \vdots \\
    1 & A_n
  \end{bmatrix},
  \qquad
  \boldsymbol{\beta} =
  \begin{pmatrix}
    \beta_0 \\
    \beta_1
  \end{pmatrix}.
\]
\end{latexcode}

\code{matrix} has no delimiters; \code{pmatrix} uses parentheses;
\code{bmatrix} uses square brackets. Ampersands separate columns and
\code{\\} separates rows. Every row should have a consistent number of
columns.

\cmd{mathbf} makes Latin letters upright bold. \cmd{boldsymbol}, supplied by
\pkg{amsmath}, also handles many Greek symbols. Vector typography varies by
discipline; define and apply one convention consistently.

\begin{commonerror}
\textbf{Symptom:} ``Extra alignment tab has been changed to \cmd{cr}.''

\textbf{Cause:} one matrix row has too many ampersands, so it declares more
columns than the structure expects.

\textbf{Repair:} count entries and ampersands in each row. A two-column row has
one ampersand.
\end{commonerror}

\section{Calculus and large operators}

\LaTeX{} source mirrors the hierarchy of standard notation:

\begin{align*}
  \frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} &= 2x, \\
  \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} &= 2x+y, \\
  \int_{0}^{T} v(t)\,\mathrm{d}t &= s(T)-s(0), \\
  \sum_{i=1}^{n}x_i &\longrightarrow n\mu, \\
  \lim_{n\to\infty}\bar{x}_n &= \mu.
\end{align*}

This book uses an upright differential \(\mathrm{d}\), with a thin space
\code{\textbackslash,} before it in an integral. That is a documented style
choice, not a universal \LaTeX{} requirement. The commands \cmd{partial},
\cmd{int}, \cmd{sum}, \cmd{lim}, \cmd{infty}, and \cmd{to} express the
symbols and operators directly.

\section{Definitions, theorems, and proofs}

Load \pkg{amsthm} and define theorem structures in the preamble, not in the
middle of the argument:

\begin{latexcode}{Preamble theorem definitions}
\usepackage{amsthm}

\theoremstyle{definition}
\newtheorem{definition}{Definition}[section]
\theoremstyle{plain}
\newtheorem{theorem}[definition]{Theorem}
\end{latexcode}

The optional \code{[section]} resets definition numbers in each section.
Sharing \code{[definition]} gives definitions and theorems one common sequence.
In the body:

\begin{definition}[Paired difference]
For paired values \(A_i\) and \(B_i\), the signed paired difference is
\(d_i=B_i-A_i\).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}
If \(A_i=B_i\) for every complete pair, then the mean signed difference is
zero.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For every complete pair, \(d_i=B_i-A_i=0\). Therefore the sum of all
differences is zero, and division by the positive number of complete pairs
gives \(\bar d=0\).
\end{proof}

The proof environment supplies its heading and end mark. A mathematical result
can be typeset elegantly and still be trivial, false, or irrelevant; proof and
peer review remain intellectual tasks.

\begin{scienceinpractice}
In computer science, a lemma may establish one property used by an algorithm's
correctness theorem. In statistics, assumptions may be stated as definitions
or conditions rather than theorems. Use theorem structures only when they
match the discipline's reasoning, not to make ordinary observations look more
authoritative.
\end{scienceinpractice}

\section{Numbering by section}

Numbering that resets by section helps readers locate results in long
mathematical documents. In a short article, a single sequence may be simpler.
The optional argument to \cmd{newtheorem} defines the relationship; do not
manually type ``Theorem 2.3''.

Labels work with theorem-like environments. Place a label immediately after
the environment begins and refer to it through the same reference system used
for equations and figures.

\begin{goodpractice}
Before choosing an elaborate notation, write down what each object means, its
dimensions, its allowed values, and the convention for bold, italic, and
upright symbols. Consistent simple notation is easier to review than ornate
notation introduced without need.
\end{goodpractice}

\begin{latexcode}{Equations, a matrix, and a theorem in one file}
\documentclass{article}
\usepackage{amsmath,amsthm}
\newtheorem{theorem}{Theorem}[section]

\begin{document}
\section{A compact model}
\begin{equation}
  A=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\3&4\end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  f(x)=\begin{cases}
    x^2, & x\geq 0,\\
    -x,  & x<0.
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}
If $n$ is even, then $n^2$ is even.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $n=2k$. Then $n^2=2(2k^2)$.
\end{proof}
\end{document}
\end{latexcode}

\chaptersummary

\code{aligned} can break one numbered derivation; \code{align} can number
separate related lines. \code{cases} expresses piecewise definitions. Matrix
environments use ampersands for columns and row breaks for rows. Structured
commands typeset calculus and large operators. \pkg{amsthm} provides semantic
definition, theorem, lemma, and proof structures whose numbering is declared
once in the preamble.
