\chapter{Introduction}
\label{chap:introduction}

Measurement methods are often compared when a new procedure is cheaper,
faster, or less invasive than an established one. A useful evaluation must
separate association from agreement and relate observed differences to a
scientifically justified tolerance.

This teaching thesis asks: what systematic difference and dispersion appear
between two simulated methods? Its objectives are to define an auditable
analysis, report units and uncertainty consistently, and distinguish computed
results from acceptable-use decisions. \Cref{chap:literature} gives context,
\cref{chap:methods} defines the analysis, and \cref{chap:results} reports the
simulated findings.
